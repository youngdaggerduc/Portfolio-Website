[TITLE: Project: Odoo ERP Rollout & Modernization]
When Pierce joined Radian, quotations and sales orders ran on an ageing Microsoft Access application whose only developer no longer supported it, and inventory and quote tracking were spread across Excel files. A quotation or sales order could take about 45–60 minutes. With roughly two months to a single go-live date, Pierce led the internal move to Odoo: he met department heads and frontline staff, observed workflows, mapped current and future processes, defined the customisation requirements, and coordinated the external developers who built the custom modules (custom rental workflow, quotation templates, per-line pricelists, sale-order line numbering, rental duration fix and others). He cleaned the legacy data (inconsistent names, spellings, abbreviations and missing fields) with Excel, AI-assisted methods and manual checks, gave data-entry training, and ran a single-date go-live with hands-on demos, one-on-one support and user guides. After go-live he made edits to several of the vendor-built modules to enhance them and align them with in-house workflows.
Odoo now covers Sales and Quotations, Rental, Inventory, Purchasing, Accounting, CRM, Reporting, Contacts, Fleet, delivery notes and picking tickets, on Odoo 19 Enterprise via Odoo.sh.
Outcome: comparable quotations went from about 45–60 minutes to about 2–8 minutes (observed times, not a formal time study), with less legacy downtime and better traceability.

[TITLE: Project: Industrial Rental Reconciliation]
A very large scaffolding rental job at an industrial plant in Trinidad produced three manually maintained spreadsheets with thousands of lines, and Radian's records and the customer's disagreed by a significant amount. Under a tight deadline Pierce cross-checked the sheets against delivery notes, picking tickets, equipment quantities, rental dates and billing rules, and recalculated the figures. He found errors on both sides, including weekend rental days the customer's reconciliation left out even though the rental policy charges for as long as material is on site. Both parties accepted his final reconciliation, and the job was closed and paid. The lessons fed into more automated rental and record workflows.

[TITLE: How Pierce Works]
1. Listen: talk to department heads and frontline staff about delays, handoffs and pain points.
2. Observe: watch how work really happens and review the forms, spreadsheets and reports behind it.
3. Map: document the current process and design a better future-state workflow.
4. Prioritise: rank by urgency and impact, and work through competing department needs with the people involved.
5. Build: configure or build the solution, often with AI-assisted development, and demo early.
6. Test and train: validate against requirements, train staff, and keep improving after rollout.
